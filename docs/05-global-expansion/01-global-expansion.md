# Dossier 5 — Global Expansion and Coverage Strategy

## 1. Objective

World Fabric expands globally without numerical quotas. Inclusion is governed by strategic relevance, stable criteria and a coverage benchmark.

The project must avoid two predictable biases:

- over-documenting data-rich regions such as Europe and East Asia;
- claiming “global” coverage while entire infrastructure families or regions remain weakly represented.

## 2. Global inclusion rule

> **An infrastructure or project is included when it materially enables, expands, redirects, substitutes, connects, coordinates or constrains international flows of goods, energy or data.**

Strategic levels:

- L1 — global structural;
- L2 — macro-regional structural;
- L3 — strategic connector.

L3 inclusion is selective: national location alone is insufficient. The object must have significant international spillover or system function.

## 3. Coverage matrix

Coverage is assessed across three axes:

1. region;
2. infrastructure family;
3. strategic level.

Each cell records:

```yaml
coverage_status: none | initial | partial | substantial | mature
objects_identified:
objects_validated:
objects_published:
priority_gaps: []
last_reviewed:
reviewer:
```

A cell cannot become `mature` based on object count alone. It must cover principal structural assets, transformative projects, known gaps, current/diverse sources, human review and minimal comparability.

## 6. Stress-test sequence

Global extension begins only after three deliberately different pilots:

### Pilot 1 — Middle Corridor
Multimodal terrestrial system. Tests border crossings, modal transfers, rail/port interfaces, uneven segment capacity, project impacts and bottleneck migration.

### Pilot 2 — Suez / Red Sea
Maritime distributed system. Tests chokepoints, rerouting, port systems, disruption, risk, insurance/security effects and resilience.

### Pilot 3 — Europe–Asia cable systems
Digital network. Tests network topology, redundancy, landing stations, route concentration, capacity visibility and the difference between physical path and service dependency.

The order is Middle Corridor → Suez/Red Sea → cables.

## 7. Expansion after pilots

Expansion uses a gap-driven queue rather than a geography-only march.

Priority function considers:

- L1 structural importance;
- major uncovered chokepoints/corridors;
- coverage-matrix gaps;
- active transformative projects;
- evidence availability;
- methodological diversity value;
- regional balance;
- dependency relevance to already documented systems.

No opaque global priority score is required. The queue can use documented criteria and human selection.

## 8. Object-discovery workflow

For each matrix cell:

1. identify known structural corridors/networks;
2. identify critical assets enabling them;
3. identify major projects altering those assets;
4. identify strategic connectors that do not belong neatly to a corridor;
5. identify missing infrastructure families;
6. compare against external infrastructure datasets and institutional maps;
7. document residual gaps.

## 9. Coverage status meaning

### none
No systematic research performed.

### initial
Candidates identified; canonical set incomplete.

### partial
Several principal objects validated, material known gaps remain.

### substantial
Most major structural objects and transformative projects covered; gaps documented.

### mature
Principal structural objects covered, projects monitored, sources current/diverse, methodology comparable and residual gaps unlikely to change the regional/family picture materially.

`mature` does not mean exhaustive.

## 10. Global public inventory outputs

World Fabric should eventually provide navigable lists of:

- structural infrastructure assets;
- strategic corridors;
- transformative projects;
- current bottlenecks;
- strategic dependencies;
- validated system transformations;
- coverage gaps.

Filters should include region, infrastructure family, L1/L2/L3 level, lifecycle status and epistemic status.

## 11. Bias controls

Monitor:

- source-language bias;
- English-language media bias;
- sponsor-data bias;
- data-rich-country bias;
- current-news bias;
- megaproject fascination;
- bias toward physical infrastructure over coordination systems;
- bias toward high-value goods while ignoring low-value strategic inputs.

A weakly documented region must be marked weakly documented, not assumed less important.

## 12. Coverage benchmark publication

Publicly expose a coverage heatmap and known-gap register. Avoid a single “World Fabric is 82% complete” number unless a defensible denominator exists.

## 13. Expansion success condition

World Fabric has achieved meaningful global coverage when every region × family × level cell has an explicit status, L1 gaps are rare and documented, and the remaining incompleteness is visible rather than hidden behind aggregate project counts.
